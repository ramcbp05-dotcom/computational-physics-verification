### Symplectic vs non-symplectic: how the error grows with run length

Both integrators at 2000 steps per orbit, `e = 0.5`:

| orbits | leapfrog dE/E | RK4 dE/E | leapfrog dL/L | RK4 dL/L |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 2.682e-05 | 1.540e-10 | 2.205e-14 | 7.177e-12 |
| 20 | 2.682e-05 | 2.932e-10 | 3.166e-14 | 2.871e-11 |
| 80 | 2.682e-05 | 8.501e-10 | 3.166e-14 | 1.149e-10 |
| 320 | 2.682e-05 | 3.078e-09 | 8.512e-14 | 4.594e-10 |

This table is the reason both integrators are kept. **Leapfrog's energy error is flat** -- a symplectic map has no secular energy drift, only a bounded oscillation of size `O(dt^2)`. **RK4's grows** with the number of steps, even though it is several orders of magnitude smaller to begin with. Angular momentum shows the same split: leapfrog holds it at round-off because every sub-step is parallel to `r` or to `v`, while RK4's drifts.

So "symplectic" and "accurate" are different properties, and which one matters depends on the run: RK4 for a short high-accuracy integration, leapfrog for a long one where the orbit must stay closed.

The spread is the report's main point. Identities that hold exactly are verified at round-off and anything larger is a bug; quantities that involve a discretisation carry an error that must shrink at the promised rate. Mixing the two up -- quoting a `1e-3` tolerance for an exact identity, or demanding `1e-15` from a finite difference -- is how a verification suite stops meaning anything.
